GOLD - CAN THE LOSSES BE LIMITED (or turned into wins)?
=======================================================
2026-10-01, follow-up to the sweep question. Everything below is measured on the real engine,
first on a validated H1 simulator (thousands of variants per minute), then on the MT5 Strategy
Tester (tick-level, authoritative) for the survivors.

1. WHERE THE LOSSES ACTUALLY ARE (2010-2026 export, 2,489 trades)
---------------------------------------------------------------
  full stop  -1R       723 trades (29%)   -722R      <- the single biggest loss bucket
  -1R to -0.6R         227 ( 9%)          -178R
  -0.6 to -0.3R        172 ( 7%)           -78R
  -0.3R to 0           395 (16%)           -28R
  wins > +3R           108 ( 4%)          +446R      <- 4% of the trades carry 35% of all profit

  Time in trade:  <1h   189 trades, avg -0.95R, win rate  1%
                  1-4h  522,        avg -0.57R, win rate 13%
                  4-12h 1093,       avg  0.00R, win rate 38%
                  12-24h 375,       avg +1.07R, win rate 68%
                  >24h  310,        avg +1.11R, win rate 74%
  Losses are born in the first hours; profit is made by trades that live a day or more.

2. METHOD
---------
  research/h1sim.py      replays the real entries on H1 bars with the EA's rules (stop, add, trail,
                         session flat). Validated against the export: +189R simulated vs +174R real
                         trade-level total for 2020-2026 (+8.5%), per-trade median difference 0.000R
                         (the earlier +116R gap was a fidelity bug: session exit one bar late, trail
                         one bar early - both fixed).
  run_cc_year.ps1        the deployed EA through the MT5 tester, one-year windows, fixed 1% risk,
                         limits 50/50, a fresh magic per run.

3. RESULTS - H1 SIMULATOR (2020-2026, 792 base entries, identical sample)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  setup                              total R    PF   win%   avg R
  live now (baseline)                 189.2    1.48   38    0.239
  no breakeven after the add          217.4    1.53   40    0.274
  stop 1.5 ATR (no BE)                263.3    1.47   36    0.332
  stop 1.25 ATR (no BE)               313.5    1.49   33    0.396
  stop 1.0 ATR (no BE)                392.5    1.52   29    0.496
  adds at +1R and +2R (no BE)         252.1    1.58   37    0.318
  time stop (out if < +0.3R after 6h) 177.1    1.52   39    0.224
  FADE the failed breakout (reverse)  -42 to -134 depending on the second trade's stop
  CONTINUATION re-entry after a stop  +247 to +291 (second trade +0.13 to +0.23R per event)

  The two structural findings:
   * after our stop is hit the price KEEPS GOING in the original direction: fading it loses
     -0.45 to -0.72R per event (win rate 4-22%), while re-entering the same way earns
     +0.13 to +0.23R per event (win rate 37-47%). "Turn the loss into a win" is possible, but the
     trade to take is the continuation, not the reversal.
   * the tight stop looked like the biggest single win in the simulator (+107R, +57%).

4. RESULTS - MT5 TESTER (tick-level, one-year windows, same config)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  year        live            stop 1.25 ATR     no BE after add
  2021      -40.4R (128)      -70.6R (163)        -39.9R (128)
  2022      -20.3R (212)      -27.4R (261)        -20.3R (212)  [identical]
  2025      +81.9R (225)     +118.2R (273)        +81.9R (225)  [identical]
  2026      +32.7R (118)      +51.4R (146)        +32.7R (118)  [identical]
  total     +53.9R            +71.6R              +53.9R

  * the tight stop is REFUTED as a loss-limiter: it is 75% worse in the worst chop year (2021),
    a bit worse in 2022, and better only in the trending years - a variance amplifier, not a
    loss reducer. The simulator's H1 path was too coarse to see the whipsaw (a 1.25-ATR stop is
    about one H1 bar range, so intrabar sequencing decides everything).
  * disabling the breakeven-after-add does nothing at all in the real engine (three years byte-
    identical): the trailing stop is already at or above the entry by the time the add fills.
  * the continuation re-entry DOES fire in the tester (17 events in 2022) but changes nothing:
    after a stop-out the EA's normal logic already takes the next breakout, so my simulated
    "extra leg" was a trade the engine already has (the simulator double counted real entries -
    a reminder that the simulator can only rank ideas, never confirm them).

5. THE SPLIT ENTRY - the one change that limits the losses without giving up the edge
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Half the position at the breakout, the second half only once the break is confirmed by another
  0.5R (1 ATR). A failed breakout (the sweep that started all of this) then costs HALF as much,
  while a confirmed one carries the full size. Implemented as InpEntryFrac=0.5 with InpAddAtR=0.5
  (each unit scales to the same % risk, so the geometry - not the size - is what changes).

  MT5 tester, one-year windows (the split variant's R is converted to the same risk unit as the
  baseline by multiplying by InpEntryFrac = 0.5, because each of its units risks half):

    year    live                             split entry (normalised)
    2021    -42.6R (131 trades)              -28.7R (165)     -33% loss
    2022    -21.3R (214)                     -11.3R (277)     -47% loss
    2023     +8.0R (155)                      +2.6R (193)     -68% profit
    2024    +21.2R (163)                     +13.9R (204)     -34% profit
    2025    +81.8R (224)                     +58.9R (273)     -28% profit
    2026    +32.7R (119)                     +21.0R (148)     -36% profit
    -------------------------------------------------------------------
    total   +79.8R                           +56.4R           -29% total

  Honest reading: this is a *de-risking dial*, not a new edge. Every window behaves proportionally
  - the losing years improve by 33-47%, the winning years give up 28-36% - so the return per unit
  of risk barely moves (total/worst-year: 1.87 -> 1.97). It does not turn the losses into wins and
  it does not beat the current engine on total profit. What it does is take half the risk on every
  *unconfirmed* breakout (the sweeps) while a confirmed one still gets the full size, which is the
  only mechanism found in ~20 variants that touches the loss side at all.
  Recommendation: worth having as an option (InpEntryFrac) rather than a default; if the daily
  drawdown or the FTMO daily limit is the binding constraint (rather than the total return), moving
  InpEntryFrac 1.0 -> 0.75 -> 0.5 is a controlled way to cut the bad days, and the size can be
  dialled back up if the return matters more than the drawdown.

6. VERDICT
----------
  * "Limit the losses" as a rule on the *exit* side does not work on this engine: every variant
    tested here (tighter stop, breakeven toggles, time stops, fade-the-sweep, second add) either
    changed nothing, made the bad years worse, or cut the tail that pays for the losses.
  * The one mechanism that does work is on the *entry* side: pay for the trade in two halves, the
    second only after the breakout proves itself. It halves the cost of the failed breakouts
    (the exact trades this file started from) and keeps the confirmed ones intact.
  * Recommended next step: run the split entry over 2023/2024/2026 too (running now) and, if it
    holds, add InpEntryFrac to the deployed EA and slowly move the live preset (InpEntryFrac 1.0
    -> 0.75 -> 0.5) while watching the fill quality of the second unit on the real account.
